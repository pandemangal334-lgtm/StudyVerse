Topic 1

Entrepreneurial Mindset

A way of thinking that helps people identify opportunities, solve problems creatively, take calculated risks and learn from success and failure.

Core traits

  • Opportunity recognition
  • Creativity, critical thinking and problem-solving
  • Leadership, confidence and decision-making
  • Willingness to learn, persistence and customer focus
  • Additional summary traits: proactiveness and adaptability
Risk-taking: deciding under uncertainty after analysing possible outcomes. Successful entrepreneurs take calculated risks, not blind risks.

Risk types: financial (money loss), market (customer rejection), operational (daily-work problems), technological (technology becomes outdated).

Risk management: market research, prototypes, MVP testing, revenue diversification, backup plans and competitor monitoring.

Resilience means recovering from setbacks and continuing to pursue goals. Adaptability means adjusting strategy, products or business models when conditions change.

PDF examples: restaurant starting online delivery during lockdown; app founders improve after low downloads. Additional summary examples include Netflix shifting from DVD rental to streaming.

Entrepreneur responsibilities: identify opportunities, arrange finance, recruit, develop and market products, generate profit.

Topic 3

Entrepreneur Self-Reflection

Evaluating strengths, weaknesses, interests, personality and readiness before starting a business.

  • Strengths: leadership, communication, programming, creativity, teamwork.
  • Weaknesses: fear of failure, poor financial knowledge, time management, low confidence.
  • Technical skills: Python, web development, AI, machine learning.
  • Business skills: marketing, finance, sales, customer management.
  • Soft skills: communication, negotiation, leadership, problem-solving.

SWOT Analysis

Strengths

Internal positives: programming, leadership.

Weaknesses

Internal limitations: limited marketing knowledge, financial management.

Opportunities

External positives: growing AI market, government schemes.

Threats

External challenges: strong competition, rapid technology changes.

Benefits: career planning, confidence, skill development, decision-making and goal setting.

A startup moves through stages to check that it is solving a real problem and can grow sustainably.

  • Problem identification: e.g. students struggle to organise lecture notes.
  • Problem–solution fit: interviews, surveys, observation and feedback to confirm the solution solves the problem.
  • MVP: simplest version to reduce cost, test assumptions and gather feedback; PDF example: basic attendance app.
  • Customer validation: collect suggestions, complaints and improvement ideas.
  • Product–market fit (PMF): product satisfies customer needs and demand; indicators include growing customers, repeat purchases, positive reviews, referrals and revenue growth.

  • Scaling: team expansion, international markets, automation and process optimisation.

Topic 5

Peer Problem Interview

A structured conversation with potential users or peers to understand real problems before building a solution. It validates assumptions using evidence instead of personal opinions.

Process

  1. Identify target users (e.g. college students).
  2. Prepare open-ended questions: “What difficulties do you face daily?”, “How do you solve this now?”, “What frustrates you most?”
  3. Conduct interviews; listen and avoid selling your idea.
  4. Record responses.
  5. Analyse patterns and find common problems.

Objectives/benefits: understand pain points, validate problems, avoid unnecessary products, gain user insights, reduce risk and improve customer satisfaction.

Topic 6

Market Opportunity Evaluation

Determines whether a business idea has sufficient demand, profitability and growth potential.

Factors

  • Market size: number of potential customers.
  • Customer demand: need for the product.
  • Competition: number and strength of competitors.
  • Growth potential: future expansion possibilities.
  • Revenue potential: expected customer income.
  • Cost analysis: development, marketing, operations and maintenance.

Tools

SWOT Analysis · PESTLE Analysis · Porter's Five Forces · Market Surveys · Competitor Analysis

TAM–SAM–SOM (in PDF summary)

TAMTotal Addressable Market — entire market demand
→
SAMServiceable Available Market — portion realistically targeted
→
SOMServiceable Obtainable Market — actual share captured

PDF example: food delivery in India — TAM: entire food industry; SAM: urban online delivery; SOM: metro-city app users.

Importance: reduces investment risk, identifies profitable opportunities, supports planning and can attract investors.

Topic 7

Customer Discovery Principles

Understanding customer problems, behaviour, needs and expectations before developing a product. It is a core concept of Lean Startup methodology.

  • Talk to customers: interview potential users regularly.
  • Validate assumptions: verify needs with evidence.
  • Observe behaviour: study how people currently solve problems.
  • Focus on problems: customers buy solutions to problems, not technology for its own sake.
  • Continuous feedback: improve based on customer opinions.
  • Get out of the building: speak directly with customers.
  • Rapid iteration: build, test, refine and pivot when evidence calls for it.

Topic 8

Value Proposition Basics

A clear statement explaining why customers should choose a product or service over alternatives. It communicates the unique value delivered.

Components

  • Customer / target customer: who has the problem?
  • Problem: what difficulty do they face?
  • Solution: how does the product help?
  • Benefits: saves time/cost, improves productivity or convenience.
  • USP / differentiator: what makes it different — faster, cheaper, more secure, easier to use or AI-powered.

PDF example: Problem: students miss assignment deadlines. Solution: AI-based academic planner with reminders.

“An intelligent academic planner that helps students manage assignments, receive smart reminders, and improve productivity with minimal effort.”

Good value proposition: clear, customer-focused, simple, specific, unique and easy to understand.

Additional summary examples: Ola (on-demand cabs for urban commuters); BYJU'S (engaging video lessons and adaptive learning for students).

Topic 9

Solution Brainstorming

A creative process for generating multiple ideas to solve a validated customer problem without immediately judging or rejecting ideas.

Process

  1. Define the problem clearly.
  2. Generate ideas freely and record every idea.
  3. Avoid criticism during idea generation.
  4. Group similar ideas.
  5. Evaluate feasibility, cost, innovation, customer value, time and technical complexity.
  6. Select the best fit for customer needs and available resources.

Techniques

Brainstorming · Mind Mapping · SCAMPER · Reverse Brainstorming · Brainwriting (6-3-5) · Design Thinking · Crazy 8s · Nominal Group Technique (NGT)

Benefits: creativity, multiple alternatives, teamwork, better decisions, reduced chance of choosing weak ideas and more practical customer-focused solutions.

Must-remember distinctions

  • Entrepreneur: independent business owner; takes business risk.
